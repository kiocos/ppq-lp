0x01 Manifesto

Three decades later, the most private thing most people write is a prompt. The symptom. The contract. The half-finished code. The question you’d never ask out loud.

Every AI router sits in the middle of that, able to read it. A promise not to look is only a policy, and policies change, leak, or get compelled.

So we stopped asking for your trust.

  1. 01

    No name required.

    Buy credits, get a credit id. That’s the whole account. Email is optional, never required.

  2. 02

    No blind faith.

    Chat runs inside an attested enclave whose code is open source and reproducibly built. Check it yourself.

  3. 03

    No leash.

    No subscription, no lock-in, hundreds of models. Top up when you want. Leave when you want.

0x02 Don’t trust. Verify.

From “we won’t look” to “we can’t.”

Every chat request, from the web app or the API, is encrypted on your device and decrypted only inside an AWS Nitro enclave. PayPerQ’s servers, staff, and logs can’t read into it. Neither can AWS.

Our backend gets a credit check and a billing event: which model, how many tokens, when. Never your prompt. Never the reply.

0x08 Limits

The fine print, in large print.

A privacy claim that hides its edges isn’t worth much. Here are ours.

  1. Media

    Media isn’t in the enclave yet.

    Image, video, music, speech, and research tools still run on our backend. We don’t log those queries, but we can’t prove it yet. Moving them into the enclave is planned.

  2. Payment

    Cards leave a trail.

    Pay by card and your card details are tied to your credit id at PayPerQ, though never at the provider. Pay with crypto if that matters to you.

  3. Network

    Your IP reaches our edge.

    Like any web request. Use a VPN if you want it hidden too.

  4. OpenAI

    OpenAI asks for a customer tag.*

    For OpenAI models, the enclave attaches a keyed hash of your credit id. OpenAI can tell requests came from the same customer, but can’t turn the hash back into a credit id, let alone a person.

  5. Trust

    This isn’t zero trust.

    You still trust that Amazon built Nitro the way it says, and the independent reviewers who checked. You no longer have to trust us.
